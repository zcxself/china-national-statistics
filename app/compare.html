China against the world’s other big economies

China is the world’s second-largest economy at market exchange rates — and the largest on a purchasing-power basis. It is growing faster than the US and Japan but slower than India, and its GDP per person ($13,293) is still a long way behind theirs.

What the comparison shows

  • Scale: the US economy ($29.30tn) is about 1.6 times China’s ($18.73tn) at market exchange rates; on a PPP basis China ($38.19tn) is larger than the US ($29.30tn).
  • People: India (1,451m) has just overtaken China (1,409m) as the world’s most populous country; Japan (124m) is shrinking too.
  • Income: GDP per capita runs from $2,592 in India to $86,170 in the US. China ($13,293) sits between Japan ($33,797) and India — an upper-middle-income economy by the World Bank’s line.
  • Growth: India grew fastest (7.1%), then China (5.0%), the US (2.8%); Japan’s economy shrank slightly (−0.2%).
  • Health: life expectancy is highest in Japan (84.0 years), then the US (78.9), China (78.0) and India (72.0).
