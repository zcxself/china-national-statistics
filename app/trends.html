Trends · 2015–2024

A decade in three lines: richer, then smaller

Between 2015 and 2024 China’s economy grew by two-thirds in dollars, its people got 62.6% richer per person, and its population rose, peaked in 2021, and then began to fall. All three series below come from the same two publications — the World Bank’s World Development Indicators (July 2026 update) for the dollar figures and mid-year population, and the NBS for the headline year-end counts.

Population, 2015–2024

Mid-year estimates; the headline 1,408.28m is the NBS year-end count.

The population rose every year to 2021, then fell in 2022, 2023 and 2024. The mid-year estimate for 2024 (1,409.0m) sits about 0.7 million above the NBS year-end count (1,408.28m) because of the date, not an error.

Source: World Bank WDI; NBS 2024 communique. More on people →

GDP per person, 2015–2024

Current US$ per person. The 2015 ($8,175) and 2024 ($13,293) endpoints are published; the years between are GDP divided by population.

GDP per person crossed $10,000 in 2018, passed $12,000 in 2021, and reached $13,293 in 2024 — 62.6% above 2015. The dip from 2022 to 2023 is the yuan’s fall against the dollar, not a fall in output: real GDP grew 5.2% that year.

Source: World Bank WDI (July 2026 update); 2016–2023 computed, see Sources.

What the decade says

Three facts from the charts above. First, the economy’s dollar size is hostage to the exchange rate: the 2020–2021 jump of $3.2 trillion came mostly from a stronger yuan, and the 2022–2023 dip came from a weaker one, while real growth stayed positive in both years. Second, the population story turned in 2021: nine years of growth, then three years of decline, and the fall is still small — 3.4 million from the peak — next to the 310 million people now aged 60 and over. Third, the per-person story is steadier than the total: GDP per capita rose in nine of the ten years.
