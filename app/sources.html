Sources & methods

Where every number comes from

This site anchors on 2024. Its national figures come from two publications, so any headline can be checked in minutes; the provincial, employment, trade and energy pages add their own named sources below. Where a figure is computed from published ones, the page says so.

1. National Bureau of Statistics of China — Statistical Communiqué on 2024 National Economic and Social Development

Published 28 February 2025. The source for the headline figures: year-end population (1,408.28m), births (9.54m) and deaths (10.93m), urbanisation (67.00%), the 60+ population (310.31m), GDP in yuan (¥1,349,084bn), real growth (5.0% and by quarter), industry value added and shares, and the growth contributions.

Coverage: mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan). Figures are preliminary estimates; the NBS revises them in later releases.

2. World Bank — World Development Indicators, July 2026 update

The source for dollar figures and cross-country comparisons: GDP in current US$ (China $18.73tn), GDP per capita ($13,293), GDP (PPP) ($38.19tn; $27,105 per person), the 2015–2024 GDP and population series, life expectancy (China 78.0 years), and every figure in the Compare table. The Trends page draws its three decade charts from these same series.

Note: an earlier release of the same dataset gave China’s 2024 GDP as $18.75tn and GDP per capita as $13,309. The July 2026 update revised both; this site uses the updated figures throughout.

3. NBS communiques 2015–2023 (for series)

Used only for the historical series: urbanisation rates 2015–2023, births and deaths 2021–2023, annual growth 2021–2023, and the 2023 year-end population (1,409.67m) and urbanisation rate (66.16%) quoted for comparison.

Each year’s communique is published the following February.

4. Provincial statistical communiques, 2024 (for the Provinces page)

Each of the 31 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities publishes its own 2024 statistical communique. Those are the source for every row of the provincial table: year-end permanent population and its change, preliminary regional GDP, real growth, and GDP per capita (GDP divided by year-end population). Dollar figures divide the yuan ones by the 2024 average exchange rate of ¥7.1217 per US$. The Regions page computes its shares and gaps from this same table — the top-5 share (39.7%), the top-10 share (61.2%), and the per-capita extremes.

Coverage: mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan). Provincial GDP is preliminary; the NBS revises it in later releases. The provincial totals do not match the national ones exactly — see “Definitions that differ” below.

5. General Administration of Customs — 2024 goods trade (for the Trade page)

Published 14 January 2025 (reported by Xinhua): total goods trade ¥43.85tn, exports ¥25.45tn (up 7.1%), imports ¥18.39tn (up 2.3%), total up 5.0%, and the eighth consecutive year as the world’s largest goods trader. Dollar figures are derived at ¥7.1217 per US$; the ¥7.06tn surplus is exports minus imports.

Goods trade only; trade in services is counted separately and is not included.

6. Employment and energy (for the Employment and Energy pages)

Employment: NBS 2024 communique — employed people (734.39m), urban employed (473.45m), new urban jobs (12.56m), surveyed unemployment (5.1%), migrant workers (299.73m). Sector shares (primary 22.2%, secondary 29.0%, tertiary 48.8%) are from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security’s 2024 statistical bulletin. Energy: NBS 2024 communique — self-sufficiency above 80%, CO₂ per ¥10,000 of GDP down 3.4%, energy per ¥10,000 of GDP down 3.8% (after deducting raw-material use and non-fossil consumption). New-energy vehicle output (13.17m) is as reported by Xinhua on the communique.

Energy totals that the communique does not publish in a comparable form (such as all-society electricity consumption) are not estimated anywhere on this site.

Definitions that differ — read this before comparing numbers

  • Two population counts. The NBS year-end figure (1,408.28m) counts permanent residents on 31 December. The World Bank series counts mid-year estimates (1,409.0m for 2024). They differ by about 0.7 million because of the date, not an error. This site labels which one each chart uses.
  • Two GDP measures. Market exchange rates ($18.73tn) value output at the rate the yuan trades at; PPP ($38.19tn) values it at what money buys locally. They answer different questions, so the site shows both and never mixes them in one chart.
  • Growth is real, levels are nominal. Growth rates (5.0%) are adjusted for prices; GDP levels ($18.73tn) are in current dollars and move with the exchange rate — which is why GDP in dollars jumped in 2021 while real growth was 8.1%.
  • Computed figures. GDP per capita for 2016–2023 is GDP divided by population from the two published series; the 2015 ($8,175) and 2024 ($13,293) endpoints are published directly. Age-band shares are the published headcounts divided by the published total. Provincial GDP per capita is provincial GDP divided by provincial year-end population; trade dollar figures divide the published yuan figures by ¥7.1217 per US$; the trade surplus is exports minus imports.
  • Provincial totals versus national totals. The 31 provinces’ population sums to 1,406.28 million against the national 1,408.28 million — the gap is mainly military personnel, counted nationally but not by any province. Their GDP sums to ¥134,031bn against the national ¥134,908bn, a 0.65% gap — the usual difference between provincial aggregation and national accounting. The site reports both and does not force them to agree.
  • Rounding. Figures are shown as published; shares may not sum to exactly 100% (industry shares sum to 100.0%; age shares to 99.9% after rounding).

What this site is not

It is an independent presentation of published statistics, not an official publication. It is not affiliated with the National Bureau of Statistics or the World Bank. Figures are as published in the sources above; if a source revises a figure, this site’s 2024 snapshot does not change.

No data is fetched from anywhere while you browse: the whole site is static files, and it works with no network connection.
