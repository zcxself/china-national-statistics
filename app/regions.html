One country, very different economies

The five largest provinces hold almost two-fifths of all provincial GDP, the richest province per person earns 4.3 times the poorest, and the fastest-growing province grows nearly three times as fast as the slowest. The gaps are the story of China’s regions — and they are all computed from the same 31-province table on the Provinces page.

What the gaps mean

Size and wealth point in different directions. The biggest economies — Guangdong, Jiangsu, Shandong — are coastal and populous; the richest per person — Beijing, Shanghai — are small and urban; the fastest growers — Tibet, Xinjiang — are western and small. And the gaps are wide even inside one country: the richest province per person earns 4.3 times the poorest. For scale, the gap between the richest and poorest of the four countries on the Compare page is wider still — the United States at $86,170 per person against India at $2,592 — but that is between countries, not within one.
